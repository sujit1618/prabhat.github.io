About the bird

How to recognise the Crested Bunting (तुरेवाला भारीट)

The Crested Bunting (तुरेवाला भारीट) belongs to the bunting family, Emberizidae, and is similar in size to a sparrow. Both males and females have a pointed crest, which is one of the most useful features to look for when identifying the bird. The male has a dark head and body, while its wings and tail are a contrasting chestnut colour. The female is olive-brown with darker streaks, and her crest is shorter than the male’s.

Its short, cone-shaped bill helps it feed on seeds. You will often see these birds searching for food on the ground rather than high in the trees. During winter, they may gather in flocks and feed alongside other buntings, finches and larks.

Natural habitat

The open landscapes it calls home

The Crested Bunting (तुरेवाला भारीट) usually lives in open, fairly dry places where grasses grow among scattered bushes and exposed rocks. Rocky hillsides, thorn scrub and grassland all provide suitable habitat. It can also be found around terraced fields, in open thorny woodland and along the edges of forests.

It occurs at a range of elevations rather than being restricted to the plains or the hills. In the Himalayas, some populations move between higher and lower ground as the seasons change.

Food habits

What it eats and how it feeds

Seeds from grasses and weeds form the main part of its diet. In cultivated areas, it also feeds on grain that has fallen to the ground. Although it is mainly a seed eater, the Crested Bunting (तुरेवाला भारीट) also takes insects, including winged termites, and has sometimes been seen catching insects in flight.

Much of its search for food takes place on or near the ground, among grasses and other low-growing plants.

Looking for it

Where you may find it in India

In India, the Crested Bunting (तुरेवाला भारीट) occurs along the lower Himalayas, including parts of Jammu, Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand, as well as in some of the nearby northern plains. It is also found in Rajasthan and Gujarat, across parts of central India, and in areas of Maharashtra and Telangana.

Farther east, its range includes West Bengal and parts of northeastern India. It is not evenly distributed across these regions, so you are more likely to find it where there are suitable grassy slopes, rocky ground and open scrub. Its presence can vary from one locality to another and may also change with the season.

अन्नाचा बराचसा शोध जमिनीवर किंवा जमिनीलगतच्या गवत आणि लहान वनस्पतींमध्ये घेतला जातो.

पूर्वेकडे पश्चिम बंगाल आणि ईशान्य भारतातील काही प्रदेशांतही तो आढळतो. गवताळ उतार, खडकाळ जमीन आणि मोकळी झुडपे असलेल्या ठिकाणी त्याचा शोध घेता येतो. वेगवेगळ्या ठिकाणी आणि ऋतूंनुसार त्याची उपस्थिती बदलते.

About the bird

How to recognise the Pallid Harrier (पांढुरका भोवत्या)

Adult male Pallid Harriers (पांढुरका भोवत्या) are pale grey, with contrasting black wedges near the tips of their wings. Females are brown and have more strongly patterned wings, while young birds often show warm rufous underparts. Their long wings and tail give them a light, agile appearance in flight.

Identifying a female or young bird takes care because several harrier species look similar. Wing shape, face pattern and the markings beneath the wings are more reliable when considered together.

Natural habitat

The places where it lives

Pallid Harriers (पांढुरका भोवत्या) favour wide, open landscapes such as grasslands, steppe, scrubby plains and farmland. They often hunt close to the ground, passing slowly over vegetation while searching for prey.

They also need quiet places to rest. Outside the breeding season, harriers may gather at communal roosts in grass or other low vegetation.

Food habits

What it eats and how it feeds

Their food includes small mammals, birds, reptiles and insects. The balance of these foods varies with the place and the prey available.

A hunting bird usually searches from the air, then turns or drops towards prey. Low, open vegetation gives it room to see and reach animals near the ground.

Looking for it

Where you may find it in India

The Pallid Harrier (पांढुरका भोवत्या) visits India mainly during the cooler months, after breeding farther north in Eurasia. It is recorded in open country across northern, western, central and parts of peninsular India.

Grassland regions in Gujarat and Rajasthan are familiar places to look for it. It also occurs farther south, including parts of Maharashtra and the Deccan. Local numbers depend on habitat, season and food availability.

About the bird

How to recognise the Red Avadavat (लाल मुनिया)

Breeding males have bright red plumage, white spots and darker wings and tail. Females are much quieter in colour, with brownish or grey upperparts and a reddish rump. Males become less colourful outside the breeding season, so a bird’s appearance can change through the year.

The small red bill and rounded tail are useful features to notice. These birds often move and feed in groups, which may be easier to spot than a single bird hidden in the grass.

Natural habitat

The places where it lives

Red Avadavats (लाल मुनिया) live in grasslands, reedbed edges and grassy clearings near villages. They also use rice fields, sugarcane fields and other cultivated areas where there are seeds and patches of cover.

Many suitable sites are close to rivers, canals or wetlands. A mixture of grasses, low bushes and nearby water provides places to feed, shelter and nest.

Food habits

What it eats and how it feeds

They feed mainly on small grass seeds, including rice and millet. A bird may pick seeds from the ground or cling to a grass stem and reach the ripening seed head.

They also take insects from time to time. Feeding flocks often work through a patch of grass together before moving to another area.

Looking for it

Where you may find it in India

The Red Avadavat (लाल मुनिया) is found across many parts of India, especially in the plains and lower country where grassy habitats and cultivation occur. Its distribution is scattered rather than continuous.

Suitable wet grasslands and field edges in northern, central, western and peninsular India can hold the species. Local presence depends strongly on the condition of the vegetation and the availability of seeds and water.

How to recognise the Common Kingfisher (छोटा खंड्या)

Its compact body, short tail and long bill give the Common Kingfisher (छोटा खंड्या) a distinctive outline. The blue on its back and wings contrasts with its warm orange breast and belly, while pale patches mark the side of its neck.

The colours can look different as the light changes. Although the plumage is bright, a still bird on a shaded waterside perch can be surprisingly difficult to notice.

Natural habitat

The places where it lives

It lives beside rivers, streams, ponds, lakes and other waters where small fish are available. It also uses some brackish and coastal waters, so it is not restricted to freshwater.

Perches overlooking the water are important for hunting. Suitable banks can provide nesting places, where the birds excavate a tunnel rather than build an exposed nest in a tree.

Food habits

What it eats and how it feeds

Small fish make up much of its food. It also takes aquatic insects and other small water animals when they are available.

A bird often watches from a perch, then dives into the water to catch prey. It returns to a perch to handle its catch before swallowing it.

Looking for it

Where you may find it in India

The Common Kingfisher (छोटा खंड्या) is widespread in India and can occur wherever suitable waterside habitat remains. It is found in the plains and in many hill regions, including streams, reservoirs and village ponds.

Local distribution depends on the water and its food supply. A particular pond or stretch of river may be used regularly, while apparently similar water nearby may have no bird present.

About the bird

How to recognise the Alexandrine Parakeet (पहाडी पोपट)

The heavy red bill, large body and long tail are useful starting points for recognising this species. A reddish-maroon patch on the shoulder is another important feature, especially when comparing it with the smaller Rose-ringed Parakeet.

Adult males have a dark and pink neck ring, while females lack the complete ring. The photograph here is a close view of a captive bird, so the full tail and shoulder markings are not visible.

Natural habitat

The places where it lives

Alexandrine Parakeets (पहाडी पोपट) use forest, woodland and other areas with mature trees. They can also occur in plantations, large gardens and wooded parts of towns where feeding and nesting opportunities remain.

Tree cavities provide nesting places. Groups may travel between feeding areas and communal resting sites, calling loudly as they fly.

Food habits

What it eats and how it feeds

They eat a variety of seeds, fruits, buds and flowers. Their strong bill helps them open and handle food, and they may use a foot to hold an item while feeding.

The foods they choose vary with the season and the trees available. They also visit cultivated areas when suitable crops or fruit are present.

Looking for it

Where you may find it in India

The Alexandrine Parakeet (पहाडी पोपट) occurs across substantial parts of India, including northern and central regions and parts of the peninsula. It is associated with wooded landscapes rather than a single type of forest.

Its presence can be very local. Mature trees, feeding opportunities and nesting cavities influence where it persists, so it is not equally common throughout its wider range.

About the bird

How to recognise the Ashy Prinia (राखी वटवट्या)

A grey head, warm buff underparts and a slender black bill give this little bird its familiar appearance. Its reddish eye and long, graduated tail are useful details to notice when it pauses on a stem.

The tail is often held upright as the bird moves through shrubbery. Northern birds can look browner outside the breeding season, and a narrow pale eyebrow may become visible.

Natural habitat

The places where it lives

Ashy Prinias (राखी वटवट्या) live in grassland, scrub and open woodland. They also make their homes along field edges and in gardens, where grasses and low bushes offer places to feed and shelter.

Tall grass, tangled shrubs and patches of fresh growth are familiar settings for this bird. It is often found singly or in pairs, moving between stems and sometimes hopping down to the ground.

Food habits

What it eats and how it feeds

The Ashy Prinia (राखी वटवट्या) feeds on insects and their larvae, including caterpillars. It searches through grasses and low vegetation for these small animals.

Its active movements are often part of this search for food. A bird may work through a patch of shrubbery before climbing onto an exposed stem.

Looking for it

Where you may find it in India

The Ashy Prinia (राखी वटवट्या) is a resident bird across much of India, from the Himalayan foothills through the plains and into peninsular India. It is familiar in many gardens and farming landscapes.

In Maharashtra it occurs widely, including around Pune. Suitable scrub and grassy patches also support it in southern India, while the driest western desert areas lie outside its usual range.

How to recognise the Black-backed Dwarf Kingfisher (तिबोटी खंड्या)

This kingfisher has a large, pointed red bill in proportion to its small body. Blue and white patches on the side of the neck break up the warm colours of the head and underparts.

It is much smaller than the familiar White-throated Kingfisher. Despite its bright plumage, the bird can be difficult to see because it often remains within dense, shaded forest.

Natural habitat

The places where it lives

It prefers shady, moist woodland and forest understorey, often near small streams. It can also occur some distance from open water, unlike kingfishers that depend mainly on fishing from exposed waterside perches.

Dense cover and quiet forest patches are important. Suitable earth banks provide places for its nesting tunnel.

Food habits

What it eats and how it feeds

It feeds on small animals such as insects, spiders, frogs and lizards. Small aquatic prey may also be taken, but fish are not its only source of food.

The bird searches from a perch and makes short movements to catch prey. Much of this feeding happens close to the forest floor or within low vegetation.

Looking for it

Where you may find it in India

In India, it occurs in parts of the Western Ghats and in northeastern forest regions. Records in western India include Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka and Kerala, where its presence and movements vary by locality.

It is particularly associated with monsoon breeding in parts of the northern Western Ghats. Its seasonal movements are still not fully understood, so local records are more useful than assuming that it is present throughout the year.

How to recognise the Common Kestrel (देव ससाणा)

Pointed wings, a long tail and rufous upperparts marked with dark spots give this bird a slender falcon shape. Adult males have a grey head and tail, while females are browner and more strongly patterned.

A perched bird may sit upright on a post, rock or tree. When it takes flight, its pointed wings and hovering behaviour can make identification easier.

Natural habitat

The places where it lives

It prefers open landscapes where prey can be seen and reached, including grassland, farmland, scrub and rocky country. It also uses some urban edges and buildings near suitable hunting areas.

Posts, trees and other exposed resting places provide lookout points. Open ground is generally more useful to it than dense, continuous forest.

Food habits

What it eats and how it feeds

Its diet includes small mammals, lizards, large insects and sometimes small birds. The mix varies with the region and the prey available.

A kestrel may hunt from a perch or search while hovering. Once it locates prey, it drops towards the ground to make the catch.

Looking for it

Where you may find it in India

The Common Kestrel (देव ससाणा) is a widespread winter visitor in India, and some populations are resident or breed locally. It occurs across much of the country in suitable open habitats, from northern regions to the peninsula.

It may be encountered around fields, dry grasslands and rocky hills. Numbers and seasonal presence differ between locations, so a familiar winter site may hold fewer birds at other times of year.

गळ्यावरील काळा भाग आणि डोळ्यांवरील गडद पट्टा या पक्ष्याच्या महत्त्वाच्या खुणा आहेत. अनेक प्रदेशांतील पक्ष्यांचा माथा तांबूस तपकिरी असतो. फिकट गाल आणि डोके व मानेभोवतीचे विरोधी रंग उठून दिसतात.

याच्या रंगछटा प्रसाराच्या वेगवेगळ्या भागांत बदलतात. लहान आकार, गोलसर शरीर आणि थव्याने फिरण्याची सवय या गोष्टी डोक्यावरील खुणांसोबत ओळख पटवण्यास मदत करतात.

नैसर्गिक अधिवास

तो कुठे राहतो?

हा डोंगराळ जंगलात आणि वृक्षसमृद्ध भागांत राहतो. रुंद पानांची जंगले, पाइनची वने आणि झुडपी वनकडा हे त्याचे अधिवास आहेत. त्याचा संबंध प्रामुख्याने झाडे असलेल्या प्रदेशांशी आहे.

अन्न शोधणारे गट वनस्पतींमधून पुढे जातात. इतर लहान वनपक्ष्यांच्या मिश्र थव्यांतही ते सामील होऊ शकतात. काही प्रदेशांतील पक्षी ऋतूनुसार उंची बदलतात.

आहाराच्या सवयी

तो काय खातो आणि अन्न कसे शोधतो?

लहान कीटक आणि कोळी हा याच्या आहाराचा महत्त्वाचा भाग आहे. तो काही बिया, लहान फळे आणि इतर वनस्पतीजन्य अन्नही खातो.

पाने, लहान काड्या आणि फांद्यांमध्ये फिरताना हे पक्षी लहान कीटक आणि इतर अन्न टिपतात. एका पक्ष्याऐवजी संपूर्ण थव्याच्या हालचालींकडे लक्ष दिल्यास त्यांचा वावर समजणे सोपे होते.

भारतात हा पक्षी प्रामुख्याने हिमालयाचा पायथा आणि डोंगराळ प्रदेशांशी संबंधित आहे. उत्तर आणि ईशान्य भारतातील या विस्तीर्ण पट्ट्यात योग्य वनप्रदेशांत तो आढळतो.

सखल मैदाने आणि द्वीपकल्पीय भारताच्या बहुतेक भागांत तो नेहमी दिसणारा पक्षी नाही. उंची, वनांचे आच्छादन आणि स्थानिक भूगोल यांनुसार त्याचा वावर बदलतो.

About the bird

How to recognise the Indian Paradise-Flycatcher (स्वर्गीय नर्तक)

The crested dark head is a useful feature in both sexes. Females are rufous above with paler underparts, while adult males can be predominantly white or rufous.

Long tail streamers develop in mature males, but not every bird has them. Young males may resemble females, so the absence of a long tail does not by itself identify a bird as female.

Natural habitat

The places where it lives

It uses forest, woodland, plantations and leafy gardens with suitable tree cover. Shaded branches provide resting places between short flights to catch insects.

The birds build a small cup nest on a branch. The photograph shows a bird feeding young at a nest.

Food habits

What it eats and how it feeds

Its food consists mainly of insects. A bird often watches from a perch and flies out to catch an insect in the air, although it can also take prey from vegetation.

It may return to a nearby branch after a feeding flight. These repeated short flights help explain why it is often noticed in the gaps between trees.

Looking for it

Where you may find it in India

The Indian Paradise-Flycatcher (स्वर्गीय नर्तक) occurs widely in suitable wooded habitats across India. Some populations are resident, while others move within the country between breeding and nonbreeding seasons.

It is found in the Himalayan foothills, central India and the peninsula. Southern India can hold both local birds and seasonal visitors, so its abundance at a particular place changes through the year.

About the bird

How to recognise the Asian Green Bee-eater (वेडा राघू)

Its plumage is mostly green, with a narrow black eye stripe, a bluish throat and a dark band below the throat. The crown and back of the neck can have a warm rusty tint.

Adults have long central tail feathers, which give the tail a delicate pointed shape. Young birds are duller and lack these long feathers, so they can look less distinctive.

Natural habitat

The places where it lives

It uses open woodland, farmland, scrub and lightly settled areas where there is room to catch flying insects. Bare branches, low wires and small trees provide useful perches.

It does not need to stay beside water and can be found in fairly dry country. Suitable earth banks and sandy ground provide places for nesting tunnels.

Food habits

What it eats and how it feeds

Flying insects make up its diet, including bees, wasps and other available prey. It watches from a perch and makes short flights to catch insects in the air.

Before swallowing a stinging insect, a bee-eater may beat and rub it against a perch. It often returns to the same area while feeding.

Looking for it

Where you may find it in India

The Asian Green Bee-eater (वेडा राघू) is widespread across the plains and lower hills of India. It can be seen in many parts of western, central, northern and peninsular India wherever suitable open habitat remains.

It is often familiar around fields, village edges and dry scrub. Although many birds are resident, local seasonal movements occur, and numbers can change with food availability and habitat conditions.
