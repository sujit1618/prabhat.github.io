Pallid Harrier

पांढुरका भोवत्या

Circus macrourus

The Pallid Harrier is a slender bird of prey that spends much of its hunting time flying low over open ground. Its long wings and buoyant flight are useful clues, although males, females and young birds can look very different from one another.

About the bird

How to recognise the Pallid Harrier

Adult male Pallid Harriers are pale grey, with contrasting black wedges near the tips of their wings. Females are brown and have more strongly patterned wings, while young birds often show warm rufous underparts. Their long wings and tail give them a light, agile appearance in flight.

Identifying a female or young bird takes care because several harrier species look similar. Wing shape, face pattern and the markings beneath the wings are more reliable when considered together.

Natural habitat

The places where it lives

Pallid Harriers favour wide, open landscapes such as grasslands, steppe, scrubby plains and farmland. They often hunt close to the ground, passing slowly over vegetation while searching for prey.

They also need quiet places to rest. Outside the breeding season, harriers may gather at communal roosts in grass or other low vegetation.

Food habits

What it eats and how it feeds

Their food includes small mammals, birds, reptiles and insects. The balance of these foods varies with the place and the prey available.

A hunting bird usually searches from the air, then turns or drops towards prey. Low, open vegetation gives it room to see and reach animals near the ground.

Where it occurs in India

Where you may find it in India

The Pallid Harrier visits India mainly during the cooler months, after breeding farther north in Eurasia. It is recorded in open country across northern, western, central and parts of peninsular India.

Grassland regions in Gujarat and Rajasthan are familiar places to look for it. It also occurs farther south, including parts of Maharashtra and the Deccan. Local numbers depend on habitat, season and food availability.
